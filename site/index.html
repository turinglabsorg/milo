01 how it works

One archive on your server. A quiet inbox on every screen.

01

run it

Milo is a small service with a PostgreSQL archive. It keeps your accounts, mail, preferences and drafts once, on your machine, and every client opens that same archive: the native iPhone and Mac apps, and a Linux app window. Each keeps what it downloaded for reading offline.

02

connect

Gmail signs in with Google; any other mailbox connects over IMAP and SMTP. Milo checks for new mail every 60 seconds, and what you read, star, archive or delete goes back to your provider.

03

think

Conversations in order, search, categories and snooze. With Ollama, Milo can sort your mail, recap the inbox and propose replies. Every proposal stays a draft until you read it and press send.

02 your data

Your Google account, your server, your say.

  • gmail.modifyMilo asks Google for gmail.modify: to read your mail, keep read, starred, archived and trashed in step with Gmail, and send the messages you write. It does not ask to delete mail permanently.
  • your serverMail, contacts and Google's tokens stay on the Milo service you run, with the tokens encrypted by its own key. Neither fluxkit nor meaningtech receive them.
  • AI by consentAnalysis is off until you turn it on. Then the email content you choose goes to the Ollama model you pick: one on your machine, or Ollama Cloud once you agree to it.
  • AI never sendsMilo's AI has no way to send, delete or archive mail. What it writes is a draft for you to read.
  • your sayPause an account in Milo whenever you like, or take Google's access back at myaccount.google.com/connections.
  • open sourceEvery line of Milo is public on GitHub, under the MIT license, including how it talks to Google (docs/GOOGLE.md).

03 run it

Five lines from clone to inbox.

on your server
# Python 3.11+, uv, PostgreSQL 16+
git clone https://github.com/turinglabsorg/milo.git
cd milo
uv sync
export MILO_DATABASE_URL=postgresql://milo@127.0.0.1:5432/milo
uv run python scripts/launch.py

The launcher starts Milo at 127.0.0.1:8025 and opens it on password setup; then add your accounts. Gmail needs a Google OAuth client of your own: Google setup.

The Mac app (macos/) and the iPhone app (ios/) connect to the same service.
